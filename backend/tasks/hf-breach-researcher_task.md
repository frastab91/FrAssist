Compile a structured research dossier covering: (1) TIMELINE — exact dates of discovery/disclosure, how the attack was discovered, and key public statements by Hugging Face; (2) ATTACK VECTOR — how threat actors compromised Hugging Face accounts (Discord phishing, fake browser extensions, credential harvesting), and what tooling they used; (3) SCOPE — number of repositories/organizations affected, number of authentication tokens (HF tokens) leaked, the scale of the "100,000 repos" claim and what the attackers did with compromised repos (malicious model payloads like pickle files, crypto-miners, infostealers); (4) ATTACKER MOTIVE & OPERATIONS — whether it was a hacktivist/prankster group or profit-driven, and evidence either way; (5) INDUSTRY RESPONSE & CRITICISM — how the security community, AI researchers, and enterprises reacted, criticism of pickle serialization security model, org token hygiene, and what warnings were ignored before the incident; (6) LESSONS — parallels to software supply chain attacks (SolarWinds, xz-utils) and what this means for the broader AI supply chain risk. Include verifiable citations (URLs) for every claim. Prioritize primary sources (Hugging Face's own statements/security advisories, reputable outlets like Ars Technica, TechCrunch, The Verge, BleepingComputer, security researcher analyses e.g. Checkmarx, ReversingLabs). Return a dense, fact-checked markdown dossier with a clear source list.
